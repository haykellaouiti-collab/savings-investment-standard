Buy or rent: the real decision criteria

Last updated: November 2026

Total cost of buying, opportunity cost of the down payment, rent growth, horizon: how to compare buying and renting honestly.

Look at the total cost of buying

The mortgage payment is only part of the cost. Purchase costs (legal, registration), upkeep, taxes, insurance and selling costs add up. An appreciating property can offset them, but not always, especially over a short period.

The opportunity cost of the down payment

Money tied up in a property is not working elsewhere. A renter can invest that sum and the monthly gap between rent and ownership costs. Our comparator applies exactly this principle: both scenarios spend the same budget and the difference is invested.

The horizon changes everything

Buying often loses in the first few years because of entry and exit costs, then becomes more attractive as the loan is repaid and the property appreciates. If you may move soon, renting keeps the advantage of flexibility.

Beyond the numbers

Stability, freedom to renovate, vacancy or repair risk and credit access are non-financial factors that also matter. Use the calculator as a guide, not a verdict.
